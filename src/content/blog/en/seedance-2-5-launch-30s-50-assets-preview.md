## Upgrade 1: 30-second native single clip

Most video models still cap a single generation around 15–20 seconds. **Seedance 2.5** pushes that to **30 seconds in one pass**—no mandatory segment-and-stitch step for a half-minute beat. For creators, that means one prompt chain can cover a full dialogue block, a product walk-through, or a short action beat instead of three separate renders plus alignment work.

Longer native clips also reduce drift at stitch points: character faces, lighting, and camera rhythm are where multi-clip pipelines usually break. If your **Seedance tutorial** workflow today is "generate 5s × 6 and pray they match," 2.5 is aimed squarely at that pain.

Reference-heavy projects pile up fast—character turnarounds, scene plates, motion refs, VO tracks, logo lockups. **Seedance 2.5** supports **up to 50 multimodal inputs in one job**, and the model is tuned to hold fine detail across that stack rather than flatten everything into a generic look.

## Upgrade 3: local edit with global consistency

Revision loops kill deadlines. **Seedance 2.5** adds **local video editing**: swap or refine a region—product label, hand pose, background prop—while the overall composition, lighting, and camera stay put. You are not re-rolling the entire 30s clip because one element missed.

For commercial work, that is post-friendly control without leaving the generator. Pair it with your existing **Seedance prompts** and reference discipline; treat local edit as a surgical pass, not a excuse to skip planning.
